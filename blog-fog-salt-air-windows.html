Fog, Salt Air & Your Windows:
A Coastal SF Survival Guide

July 7, 2026 6 min read By SF Window Station

If you live west of Twin Peaks (the Outer Sunset, the Richmond, Ocean Beach, Daly City's western edge), your windows live a harder life than windows anywhere else in the Bay Area. Persistent fog keeps them wet for hours a day, salt-laden wind attacks metal and seals, and the temperature rarely swings enough to dry things out.

We've replaced thousands of windows in these neighborhoods since 1995. Here is what the coastal microclimate actually does to windows, which materials survive it, and the maintenance that buys you years.

What the Coast Does to Windows

Salt corrosion on metal parts

Salt aerosol carried in fog settles on every exterior surface within roughly two miles of the ocean. On windows, it attacks aluminum frames (pitting and white oxidation), steel fasteners and hinges, and the metal spacers inside glass units. Original 1950s–70s aluminum sliders (still common in Sunset District homes) often corrode until the sashes barely move.

Chronic moisture and seal failure

A double-pane window's insulating seal is rated for thousands of wet-dry cycles. Coastal fog changes the math: frames stay damp for long stretches, and the sealant ages faster. That's why foggy-glass windows (failed seals, condensation trapped between panes) show up noticeably earlier near the beach, often at 10–15 years instead of 20+. If you're seeing this, our repair-or-replace guide explains when a failed seal justifies full replacement.

Swollen and rotting wood

Unclad wood windows need vigilant paint maintenance near the coast. Once paint cracks, the constantly damp marine layer feeds rot quickly. Sills and lower sash rails go first. West-of-Sunset Victorians with original wood windows can absolutely keep them, but only with a disciplined repaint cycle (more below).

Hardware Matters More Than You Think

Near the ocean, the frame usually outlives the hardware. When comparing quotes, ask specifically about:

  • Stainless steel locks, hinges, and balances: standard zinc-plated hardware rusts within a few years at the beach
  • Composite or stainless rollers on sliding windows and patio doors
  • Warm-edge, non-metal glass spacers: they resist both corrosion and condensation
  • Marine-grade exterior finishes on any clad or aluminum product

The Coastal Maintenance Schedule

Whatever windows you have today, this routine measurably extends their life:

  • Twice a year: rinse frames and tracks with fresh water, a hose with gentle pressure is enough. This alone removes most accumulated salt.
  • Yearly: wipe weatherstripping clean and check it for brittleness; lubricate locks and rollers with silicone (not oil, which traps grit).
  • Yearly: inspect exterior caulking; recaulk gaps before winter storms arrive.
  • Every 3–5 years (wood only): repaint before you see failure. By the time paint visibly peels near the coast, water is already in the wood.

The Bottom Line

Coastal San Francisco is one of the hardest window environments in California, but it's a solved problem: fiberglass or quality vinyl frames, stainless hardware, warm-edge spacers, and a twice-yearly rinse. If your current windows are corroded aluminum or fogged double-panes, replacement pays for itself in comfort immediately. These are exactly the homes where we hear "the house finally feels warm" at the final walkthrough.
