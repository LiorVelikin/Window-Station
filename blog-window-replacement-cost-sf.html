How Much Does Window Replacement Cost
in San Francisco? (2025 Guide)

June 10, 2026 7 min read By SF Window Station

Window replacement in San Francisco costs more than the national average — typically 20–35% more. Labor rates are higher, some neighborhoods have permit requirements, and a large share of the housing stock is older, which adds installation complexity. This guide gives you real numbers from Bay Area projects so you know what to expect before you call anyone.

Average Cost Per Window in San Francisco (Installed)

These ranges reflect the total installed cost — product plus labor — for a standard-size double-hung or casement window. Prices assume a one-story home with straightforward access.

MaterialCost Per Window (Installed)
Vinyl double-pane$500 – $900
Fiberglass double-pane$900 – $1,600
Clad-wood (aluminum exterior)$1,200 – $2,200
Full wood (mahogany, fir)$1,500 – $3,000+
Aluminum (residential)$700 – $1,300

Bay windows, picture windows, and specialty shapes cost significantly more due to custom sizing and the structural complexity of the installation. A bay window replacement typically runs $1,800–$5,000 depending on size and material.

What Drives Window Replacement Costs Higher in San Francisco

Number of stories

A two-story home adds significant labor cost. Installers need scaffolding or ladders, work takes longer, and there is more risk. Expect to add $80–$150 per window for second-floor access, and more for third-floor Victorian flats.

Historic district requirements

Many San Francisco neighborhoods — including parts of Haight-Ashbury, Pacific Heights, Noe Valley, and the Mission — fall under design review or historic preservation rules. In these areas, you may be required to use windows with specific profiles, divided lights, or wood or clad-wood materials. This rules out basic vinyl and adds cost.

Building permits

Most like-for-like window replacements in SF do not require a permit. However, if you are changing the size or location of a window, adding a window where none existed, or working in a landmark or conservation district, a permit is required. Permit costs vary but typically add $200–$800 to the project.

Existing frame and trim condition

Older Bay Area homes often have deteriorated sills, soft wood trim, or damaged stucco around window openings. If the existing frame needs remediation before a new window can be set, labor costs increase. We find this in roughly 30–40% of older SF homes.

How to Reduce Your Window Replacement Cost

Replace multiple windows in one project

Per-window labor costs drop when a contractor is already mobilized at your property. Replacing 10 windows at once costs meaningfully less per window than replacing them one at a time over several years. If you are going to replace more than 3–4 windows, doing them together almost always makes financial sense.

Claim the federal Energy Star tax credit

The federal 25C tax credit allows you to deduct 30% of the cost of qualifying windows (product cost only, not installation), up to $600 per year. Over two tax years, that is up to $1,200 back in your pocket. All Energy Star Zone 3-certified windows qualify. Read the full guide to California energy rebates.

Ask about off-season pricing

November through February is slower for window contractors in the Bay Area. Some installers offer 5–10% discounts during this period. It is worth asking — the weather in the Bay Area is mild enough year-round that installation quality is not affected.

Get three written quotes

Pricing varies considerably between contractors. A legitimate written quote from a licensed California contractor (CA Lic #) breaks down product, labor, and any additional costs separately. Be cautious of quotes that are significantly lower than the range above — they often involve unlicensed subcontractors or lower-grade products than quoted.

What You Should Ask Any Window Contractor

  • Is your CA contractor license current? (Verify at cslb.ca.gov)
  • Are you carrying general liability and workers comp insurance?
  • What brand and product line are you quoting, and can I see the NFRC label?
  • Does this price include haul-away of the old windows?
  • What is the warranty — on the product and on your installation labor?
  • Will this project require a permit, and if so, are you pulling it?
